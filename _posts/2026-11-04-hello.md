Most of my work ends up inside someone else's operations. At Gradyent I build forecasting and optimisation models for energy systems. Before that, during my PhD at TU Delft, I worked on state estimation and forecasting for lithium-ion batteries.

In both settings, a large part of what makes a model work never appears in the final paper or client report. It is in the data cleaning decisions, the assumptions that had to be revised once real measurements came in, and the methods that looked good in simulation and failed on field data. This blog is where I want to write those things down.

There are two reasons. Writing something out is the most reliable way I know to check whether I understand it. And short, concrete write-ups by people who have actually tried a method are often more useful than the polished version in a journal.

## What I plan to write about

**Forecasting and estimation in practice.** Time-series forecasting, moving horizon estimation, Kalman filtering and physics-informed machine learning, with attention to how they behave on sparse and noisy data rather than clean benchmarks.

**Batteries and electricity markets.** How a battery earns money across day-ahead, intraday and balancing markets, and how to bid without running it empty. The first post on this is about [bidding a battery into the Finnish mFRR market]({{ '/blog/2026/11/bidding-a-battery-into-mfrr/' | relative_url }}).

Posts will be technical, with equations and code where they help. The views here are my own and not those of my employer.
